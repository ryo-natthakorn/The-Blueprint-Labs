The Quarterly Preserve Issue 12 · Autumn

Marmalade, and other slow arguments

On bitter oranges, patient sugar, and why the best things in a kitchen refuse to be hurried.

There is a week in late winter when the bitter oranges arrive and the kitchen stops pretending to be modern. The fruit is ugly, thick-skinned and sour enough to make you laugh, and every year it asks the same question: how long are you willing to wait for something good?

The answer is longer than you think. Peel is sliced by hand, thin as a fingernail, and left overnight to soften in its own juice. Sugar goes in only when the pith has given up its bitterness, and then the pot is watched — not stirred — until a spoonful wrinkles on a cold saucer.

“Set is a moment, not a temperature.”

Type works the same way. A letter drawn for a poster has to be thinned and opened before it can whisper in a footnote, and the work is invisible when it is done well. You only notice it when someone forgot.

So we keep a jar on the studio shelf, labelled with the year, as a reminder that slow things are still allowed. Some of them even keep.
