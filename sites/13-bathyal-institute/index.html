    The ocean is a building
    six kilometres tall.

    Bathyal runs crewed and autonomous descents through every floor of it. This page is one continuous dive — scroll, and mind the pressure.

    −150 m · Epipelagic

    The last of the weather. A bait ball turns in the light; everything below here runs on what sinks from it.

    −700 m · Mesopelagic

    The twilight zone. Our acoustic arrays log the largest animal migration on Earth here, nightly, in the dark. The jellies stay.

    −1,600 m · Bathypelagic

    No sunlight has ever reached this water. Four out of five animals here make their own. Your pointer is our floodlight — or switch it off, and make your own light.

    −3,000 m · Abyssal approach

    Three hundred atmospheres. Something here fishes with a lamp of its own, and it is not afraid of ours.

    −4,500 m · Something large

    The cameras catch a silhouette perhaps once a season. Sweep the light slowly. It has the largest eyes in the animal kingdom, and it has already seen us.

    −6,000 m · Station VI

    A hydrothermal vent field and the institute's deepest lab. Chemosynthesis, tube worms, water at 380 °C that cannot boil. This is the ground floor of the planet.
